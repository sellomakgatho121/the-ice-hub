Material proof sheet · not the site

Cold, lit from behind.

Every surface shows light that has already passed through it. Entry edge narrow and bright, exit edge wider and dimmer. Judging this in words is pointless — here it is in pixels.

The reel — 20 frames, the hero asset

Every frame below is the shipped file, byte-identical to the source, no re-encode. This is the scrub sequence: 572 KB total against a 600 KB ceiling.

Still → peak → settle

Motion is front-loaded. Frames 01–07 barely change, the sequence accelerates hard from frame 08, peaks around 11–13, then settles. Quartiles measured [7.8, 20.0, 21.0, 15.0].

Cut bridged

The source has a hard cut at frame 38→39 (step 49.2, ~3× the mean). The 20-frame subset keeps 38 and drops 39, so the scrub reads smoother rather than stuttering.

No quality loss

Byte-identical copies. The 40→20 reduction is temporal, not spatial — same pixels, half the frames, a slower and calmer scrub.

02 · Glass — floating surfaces

Two edges, not one.

The top border is crisp and bright because light is arriving. The bottom border is double width, dimmer and cooler, because light is leaving after losing coherence.

The fill is deliberately uniform — an earlier draft used a diagonal gradient, which put its brightest stop at the upper-left and reintroduced exactly the key light this concept rejects. Measured separation between entry and exit edge: ΔE76 10.03, well above the ~2.3 perceptual threshold.

03 · Etched — quiet planes

Higher blur, no rim at all, lower contrast. Where Glass says "this is an active control," Etched says "this is space." Text sits beside the plane, never on it.

Focus & the order action

Tab to either button. Focus is a ring, never a wash: the widest wash step reaches only 1.28:1 against its surface, and WCAG 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for state indicators. Amber fill with abyss text measures 9.17:1.
